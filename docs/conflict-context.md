# Conflict Context — Kashmir's Mental-Health Footprint

The epidemiological and structural backdrop the product must be built around. Figures
are cited point-in-time; treat them as orienting magnitudes, not live statistics.

---

## 1. Trauma prevalence

**Kashmir Mental Health Survey 2015 ("Muntazar")** — MSF / University of Kashmir Dept of
Psychology / IMHANS; peer-reviewed (PMC5654454). Representative cross-sectional survey:
**5,428 households in 399 villages across all 10 Valley districts** (5,519 adults), Oct–
Dec 2015, using culturally adapted HSCL-25 and HTQ-16.

- **~45% adult prevalence of mental distress** (95% CI 42.6–47.0) — ~1.8 million adults.
- **~41% probable depression**, **~26% probable anxiety**, **~19% probable PTSD**.
- District depression prevalence ranged **28% (Srinagar) to 54% (Badgam)**.
- The average adult had witnessed/experienced **7.7 lifetime traumatic events**, with a
  dose–response relationship to all three disorders. Higher risk: female, >55 years, no
  formal education, rural, widowed/divorced/separated.

Earlier work: Margoob & Ahmad (2006) PTSD point prevalence 7% / lifetime 15%; de Jong et
al. (2005–06) 33% psychological distress, ~one-third with suicidal ideation. A
NIMHANS/ActionAid-cited figure: 11.3% of J&K adults with mental illness vs 7.3%
national.

**Specific populations** with elevated, often untreated distress: **half-widows** (wives
of the disappeared, in legal/marital limbo), enforced-disappearance families, pellet-gun
injury survivors, and torture survivors.

IMHANS OPD presentations rose from ~100/week (1980) to 200–300/day (2013); suicide
attempts up >250% between 1994 and 2012.

## 2. Post-2019 (Article 370) and after

The 5 Aug 2019 abrogation came with a communications blackout: per Internet Society
Pulse, **213 days of no internet and 550 days of partial/no connectivity** — the
**longest shutdown in a democracy** at the time; TIME framed it as **"the world's longest
internet shutdown — 552 days,"** with high-speed 4G restored only **5 Feb 2021**.

Documented effects: panic attacks, worsening of pre-existing conditions, disrupted
tele-psychiatry, hampered medical services (PUCL 2019; JKCCS "Kashmir's Internet Siege"
2020) — compounded by the COVID-19 lockdown and 2G-only constraints crippling online
education and telehealth.

2024–2026: J&K Assembly elections held 2024 (first since reorganization); a "tourism
normalization" narrative coexists with continued security pressure. The **April 2025
Pahalgam attack** and aftermath triggered fresh collective grief/fear and a documented
surge in harassment of Kashmiri students elsewhere in India, with NGOs mobilizing pro
bono trauma support.

> **Design consequence:** offline-capable, 2G-tolerant design is not optional — it is a
> direct response to the single most distinctive feature of this context. See
> `docs/language-access.md`.

## 3. Drug-addiction crisis

The J&K government told the Legislative Assembly (**19 Feb 2026**) that **~70,000 youth
use drugs, ~50,000 addicted to heroin** (many intravenous) — from a 2022 joint
Health/Social Welfare survey across 10 Kashmir districts. J&K is among the highest for
heroin-use prevalence in India.

- IMHANS: ~4,000 addicts/year (~90% hard drugs/heroin); a shift from cannabis/medicinal
  opioids to **starting directly with heroin**, and from oral to **injectable** use. GMC
  Srinagar de-addiction centre: 3,036 new addicts Mar 2022–Mar 2023; 38,074 follow-ups.
- Response: ATFs at GMC Srinagar/Anantnag/Handwara, OST, de-addiction OPD in all 20
  districts, IPD in all 9 GMCs; J&K Anti-Narcotics Task Force (2020); Nasha Mukti
  helpline 1800-11-0031; national MANAS/NCB 1933.
- Drivers: conflict trauma, youth unemployment, easy heroin availability.

## 4. Workforce & structural constraints

- **~0.75 psychiatrists per 100,000** in India (Garg, Brar et al., *Indian J.
  Psychiatry* 2019, cited by PIB/MoHFW) against the **WHO minimum of 3**; a 2023
  Parliamentary Standing Committee found only ~9,000 practising psychiatrists
  nationwide. Kashmir has one of the lowest ratios, worsened during shutdowns. Until Dec
  2019 the Srinagar de-addiction centre was the **only** such facility in Kashmir.
- **Internet shutdowns** → the strongest argument for offline-capable, low-bandwidth,
  SMS/lightweight-text design with cached crisis resources.
- **Seasonal isolation ("wande"):** harsh winters increase isolation and impede travel
  to services — favoring remote/async support, but colliding with shutdown risk.
- **Suicide trends:** historically rising attempts (>250%, 1994–2012), youth and women
  at notable risk; Tele-MANAS J&K reported (point-in-time) 799 suicidal-ideation/attempt
  calls and 890 averted attempts.

> A thin human workforce + a heavy burden + frequent loss of connectivity is the precise
> niche a safe, offline-capable, referral-first companion is meant to fill — **augmenting**
> scarce human services, never substituting for them.
