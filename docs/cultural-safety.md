# Cultural Safety — Kashmir

These dynamics are not "nice to have" context; they determine whether the tool helps or
harms. The system prompt encodes the operational rules; this document is the rationale.

---

## 1. Joint-family systems — support *and* stressor

Kashmiri households are typically patrilocal joint/extended families; authority
concentrates in elder males and the mother-in-law. The **saas–bahu** (mother-in-law /
daughter-in-law) dynamic is a documented locus of chronic stress for married women,
with the husband/son triangulated between them. Multigenerational households can be
**protective** (support, less isolation) *and* a source of **coercive control,
surveillance, and loss of autonomy** — especially for young brides.

**Rule:** never reflexively advise "talk to your family." Validate intra-family power
dynamics. Do not assume family is a safe disclosure target.

## 2. Izzat (honour) & "log kya kahenge"

Honour and fear of social judgment strongly regulate behaviour, especially for women.
They **suppress help-seeking** (a psychiatrist implies "pagal"/madness, threatening
reputation and marriage prospects), **discourage disclosure** of abuse, and drive
**somatic** presentation of distress (e.g., grief surfacing as toothache/back pain).

**Rule:** lead with privacy/anonymity; normalize distress as common and non-shameful;
frame help-seeking as strength; treat somatic complaints as real and feeling-linked.

## 3. Faith — the "both/and" stance

Pirs/faith healers and dargah/shrine-based healing are frequently the **first** point
of contact for distress. Islamic coping concepts — **sabr** (patience), **tawakkul**
(trust in God), Islamically-integrated CBT — carry real meaning.

The model to emulate is **Dava–Dua** (Altruist NGO, founder Milesh Hamlai), running at
the Mira Datar Dargah, Unava (Mehsana) since July 2008 with Gujarat Health Dept funding:
it trains **mujawars** (shrine officiants) to identify serious disorders and refer to
on-site psychiatrists — pairing medicine ("dava") with prayer ("dua"). Reported to have
treated **>1.25 lakh (125,000) patients across 22 states**, 800+ mujawars trained; a
peer-reviewed analysis (J. Family Medicine & Primary Care, 2021, PMC8132752) found the
combined approach patient-centred and beneficial where access is otherwise limited.

**Rule:** never denigrate faith or faith-healing. Integrate sabr/tawakkul respectfully.
Position professional help as **complementary to** faith, never a replacement.

## 4. Coercive control & domestic violence

Extended-family (in-law) involvement in domestic violence is a documented South Asian
pattern (dowry/financial coercion, controlling mother-in-law, husband triangulation).
The **MentalManip** taxonomy (gaslighting, guilt-tripping, etc.) maps onto these and can
inform a manipulation-recognition module. Relevant legal/cultural frames:

- **Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act 2005** — civil remedies (protection,
  residence, monetary relief; covers emotional/economic abuse).
- Muslim personal-law concepts: **mahr** (dower), **nafaqah** (maintenance), right to
  separate accommodation, **khula** (woman-initiated divorce).

**Rule:** recognize coercive-control patterns, help **safety-plan**, and refer
(181 / Sakhi OSC / police 112 / legal aid 15100) — **never** instruct the user to
confront the abuser.

## 5. Stigma, somatization, scalable models

The "pagal" label, fear that psychiatric records harm marriage prospects, and
somatization are well-documented in South Asia. Evidence-based, lay-delivered models to
draw tone and structure from:

- **Thinking Healthy Programme (THP)** — WHO-endorsed, CBT-based, lay/CHW-delivered for
  perinatal depression (developed in Pakistan; halved perinatal depression in the
  Rawalpindi trial; peer/tech-assisted adaptations exist).
- **WHO Problem Management Plus (PM+)** — low-intensity, lay-deliverable.
- **Sangath** (India) — lay-counsellor models.

**Rule:** psychoeducation that reframes somatic symptoms as stress-linked; a low-
intensity, peer-aligned, culturally-adapted tone — not high-jargon clinical talk.

## Cross-border helpline context (design reference only)

Pakistani Urdu-language lines show that scaled chat/WhatsApp crisis support works:
**Umang** (0311-7786264, 24/7), **Rozan** (since 2001), **Taskeen** (with a 24/7
chatbot). Useful as *design references*; for **referral**, route users to the verified
**Indian/J&K** resources in `config/crisis-resources.json`.
